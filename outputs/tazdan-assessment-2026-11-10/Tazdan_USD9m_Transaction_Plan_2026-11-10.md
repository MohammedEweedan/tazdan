# Tazdan: plan to pursue a US$9 million strategic transaction

Prepared 10 November 2026. Founder target: US$4 million gross personal proceeds plus US$5 million new company capital, for an aggregate investor holding of 33% after completion.

This is a proposed sale strategy and negotiation brief. No buyer, financing commitment, public-sector award, approved integration or licence has been secured through this work. The earlier evidence-based assessment remains unchanged: present evidence does not establish this asking price. All deal amounts are USD unless marked otherwise.

## 1. The route to pursue

Prioritise a strategic investment paired with a separately documented payroll/payment deployment. Seek a lead bank, payments operator or telecom group that can supply distribution and operational capacity, alongside a capital partner if needed. The value case must describe the incremental economics and time saved for that specific buyer.

The proposed product is an approved interface connecting public-employer payroll operations, employee payment access, bank channels and merchant reconciliation. Existing banks retain their accounts and regulated roles. Any Tazdan-custodied salary balance requires a separately approved arrangement. Technical and commercial access to LYPay and OnePay must each be established; no universal bridging ability is asserted.

CBL already has a salary initiative. Its 28 September 2026 announcement reported approximately 1.75 million registered public employees and described Ministry of Finance authorisations being transferred to commercial banks. This is evidence of an existing programme, not an audience Tazdan owns. The pitch must identify the unresolved workflow or user problem Tazdan can solve. [CBL salary announcement](https://cbl.gov.ly/en/the-central-bank-of-libya-announces-the-commencement-of-september-salary-payments-through-the-your-salary-instantly-system/)

CBL's inclusion strategy includes digital government payments and interoperability agreements. That supports thematic alignment, not an award or commitment to Tazdan. [CBL Financial Inclusion Strategy, printed page 44](https://cbl.gov.ly/micifaf/2025/11/Libyan-Financial-Inclusion-Strategy.pdf)

## 2. Primary proposal: all cash at closing

| Item | Proposed amount or treatment |

| Newly issued company equity | $5,000,000 paid into the company |
| Founder shares purchased | $4,000,000 paid to the founder, gross before tax and costs |
| Investor ownership after closing | 33% fully diluted on the expressly agreed cap table |
| Founder ownership after closing | 67%, only if initially sole owner and no other claims/pool |
| Implied post-money equity value | $27,272,727 |
| Implied pre-money equity value | $22,272,727 |
| Investor ownership attributable to primary funding | 18.3333% |
| Investor ownership attributable to secondary purchase | 14.6667% |

Arithmetic assumes the same per-share price and equivalent economics for primary and secondary shares, with no options, debt conversion, preference adjustments or fees. Actual cap-table and rights diligence is required. Primary proceeds fund the company; secondary proceeds buy existing founder shares. [Primary/secondary distinction](https://carta.com/learn/equity/liquidity-events/secondary-transactions/)

**Conditions that could make this ask more credible:**

- An executed paid deployment or minimum-purchase agreement with an authorised, budget-owning counterparty. Define committed fees, collection dates, employee/agency scope, acceptance, termination, support and implementation obligations. Forecast reach and nonbinding letters are separate from contracted minimums.
- Written confirmation of the permitted product and access model, sponsor-bank responsibilities, settlement terms, safeguarding and each rail's integration requirements. A relationship or public API document is insufficient.
- A complete delivery team and costed implementation plan; source/IP ownership and transferable rights; independently assessed security, reconciliation and recovery capability.
- A buyer-specific model showing where its returns come from: its equity economics plus independently evidenced distribution, operating or commercial benefits. Avoid counting the same benefit twice.
- A price acceptable after transparent disclosure that $4 million, or 44.4% of the check, is founder liquidity. Do not relabel that payout as regulatory capital, software implementation expense or company runway.

The $5 million capital allocation is an investment target, not a verified cost requirement. National deployment could require substantially more than the earlier narrow pilot plan, but actual regulatory capital, guarantees, reserve requirements and supplier quotes remain unknown. Unspent investment remains company cash. A larger funding requirement does not by itself establish a higher value for the existing shares.

## 3. A contingent alternative, if timing can change

Keep the full $9 million target and the $4 million/$5 million split. Reduce the buyer's initial dollars at risk through objective implementation and commercial gates.

| Proposed payment event | Company receives | Founder receives | Total paid at event | Cumulative total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Initial closing after agreed diligence and funded pilot scope | $1.5m | $0.5m | $2m | $2m |
| Acceptance of approved live integrations and pilot deliverables | $1.5m | $1.5m | $3m | $5m |
| Agreed expansion and collected-revenue milestone | $2m | $2m | $4m | $9m |
| Total | $5m | $4m | $9m | $9m |

These amounts and gates are negotiation proposals, not observed buyer preferences. Even the initial $2 million is not supported by an existing offer. Staging limits the initial commitment at risk; it does not prove the underlying price.

Specify milestones numerically before signing: approved counterparties and rails; accepted settlement and reconciliation tests; cohort size; payment success; collected fees; bounded support cost; recovery evidence; dates and cure periods. Do not make payment depend solely on the buyer's subjective satisfaction or on a vague promise to introduce someone.

Distinguish three different outcomes:

1. Cash paid at closing is received cash.
2. A fixed deferred obligation is future credit exposure and needs agreed payment protection.
3. A performance earn-out is contingent; it may never be paid. An optional follow-on investment is also not committed cash.

Do not describe a maximum $9 million contingent headline as $9 million raised or $4 million received personally. If the founder requires the entire $4 million immediately, this staged structure does not satisfy that requirement.

Issue/transfer shares only in accordance with funded stages and the agreed remedies. Do not give the full 33% for the first $2 million. Assuming the exact same fixed share price as the all-at-once proposal and no other cap-table changes, aggregate investor stakes after the three stages would be approximately 8.4130%, 19.7842% and 33.0000%, respectively. They are not simply stage cash divided by the final post-money value, because later primary issues change the denominator.

Formula: investor ownership at each stage = cumulative investor cash / ($22.272727m initial pre-money + cumulative primary capital), under the stated identical-price assumptions. Later dilution must be expressly modeled. Share issuance, escrow and enforcement mechanics require transaction documentation.

Avoid giving an unfunded buyer a long, cheap option over future equity while blocking all other financing. Any reserved future pricing needs a defined commitment, funding dates, expiry and consequences for non-payment.

## 5. Revenue that could support a serious buyer discussion

The following is a reverse calculation, not a forecast or evidence of accepted pricing. To retain $3 million of annual service revenue after partner pass-through costs from payroll alone:

| Successfully served employees every month | Monthly retained revenue required per employee, USD-equivalent |
| ---: | ---: |
| 100,000 | $2.50 |
| 250,000 | $1.00 |
| 500,000 | $0.50 |
| 1,000,000 | $0.25 |

Calculation: $3,000,000 / (employees x 12). This is revenue before Tazdan's own delivery costs, staff, security, losses, tax and capital needs. Gross contract pricing must cover partner charges too. Annual platform fees can replace or supplement per-employee fees, but cannot be counted again for the same contractual service. Neither $3 million revenue nor any employee count establishes $22.27 million pre-money on its own.

If domestic fees are in LYD and the investment/desired exit are in USD, model conversion, transferability, collection timing and exchange exposure using verified contract/bank terms. No assumed exchange rate or guaranteed USD return is embedded here. Registered employees are not the same as employees successfully served or people actively spending in the app.

A separate buyer-value illustration: five equal year-end annual cash benefits of approximately $3.347 million have a $9 million present value at an assumed 25% discount rate, with no terminal value. Both the period and rate are analytical assumptions, not local market benchmarks. These must be benefits accruing to the buyer after attributable costs. If the only benefits are proportional distributions from its 33% Tazdan ownership, the company would need approximately $10.14 million annual distributable cash under that simplified test. Do not attribute all company earnings to a minority investor.

The point is to establish a specific buyer's economics. Government wage-bill totals, safeguarded balances and headline payment volume cannot substitute for retained revenue or buyer cash benefits.

## 6. Immediate execution sequence

### Prepare and qualify, starting now

- Confirm the cap table, IP assignment, accounts, taxes and liabilities. Update the existing data room to reflect current sandbox/stub limitations. Do not reuse older claims of national readiness or production external providers.
- Produce one demonstration: payroll approval, payment execution status, employee access and employer reconciliation. Existing Tazdan-wallet payouts are a foundation, not a live national bank-payroll implementation.
- Ask the existing merger contact for a written team commitment and introduction to the SF investor's actual decision-maker. Obtain the team's roles, compensation, contributed IP, time commitment and liabilities before pricing merger equity.
- Ask the CBL contact for the formal integration/product-scope route, relevant technical authority and permissible public-employer pilot process. A meeting should lead to a written process or requirement; do not market a conversation as approval.
- Qualify potential strategic leads on authority, available funds, commercial benefit and ability to sponsor implementation. Build several independent discussions to obtain price discovery.

### Convert one specific deployment into a contract

- Select one willing public employer or other institutional payroll buyer and a sponsor bank. Identify who has authority and budget to purchase the service. CBL approval and government procurement are distinct dependencies.
- Agree paid pilot scope, current system dependencies, data access/consent, who holds money, fees, collection, acceptance tests and expansion terms.
- Obtain rail/vendor quotes and a binding team plan. Commission the necessary independent technical work. Do not promise a regulator or government award within a fixed number of weeks.
- Use collected pilot revenue and written commitments to revise the business case, capital budget and strategic term sheet.

### Negotiate the $9 million transaction

- Seek the all-cash structure first if immediate liquidity is essential. Require a funded buyer, an exact primary/secondary split and clear closing conditions.
- Compare any staged offer by cash actually paid, enforceable remaining obligations, milestone risk, dilution and restrictions on alternative finance.
- Negotiate board/veto rights, preferences, founder service obligations, further dilution and permitted commercial rights together. A 33% economic label alone does not establish control or equivalent value.
- Keep customer service agreements distinct from equity. Do not count the same dollar as both commercial revenue and investment capital; avoid granting indefinite exclusive commercial rights without pricing their cost to Tazdan's other business.

CBL publishes a supplier-registration route requesting company, financial and tax documents. Its page expressly says registration is not qualification or approval. This can help assemble a procurement package where applicable, but it is not a financial-services licence or an award. [CBL supplier registration](https://cbl.gov.ly/en/registering-suppliers/)
