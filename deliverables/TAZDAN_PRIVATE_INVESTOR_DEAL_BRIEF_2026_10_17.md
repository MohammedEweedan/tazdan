# Tazdan — qualify existing investors and test the $18m proposal

17 October 2026 · Private internal working paper · Non-binding · USD

## Current position

The founder confirms ownership of all code/IP and reports interest from wealthy individuals, including state and militia leaders. He has not told them the proposed $18m total package, comprising $8m personal share-sale proceeds and $10m company funding. He also reports a contact with strong CBL and other relationships who can assist with launch dealings.

These statements support an immediate investor-qualification process. They do not yet establish an agreed price, source of funds, investment authority, institutional backing, regulatory approval or a closing probability. No investor names, bank records, sanctions matches, offers or investment vehicles have been reviewed. The individuals are prospective financial investors unless their actual proposals establish a different role; they are not automatically strategic acquirers or software customers.

Ownership is recorded as **founder-confirmed**. Prepare evidence of authorship/assignments where applicable, the legal holder of the rights and any existing grants or encumbrances. Identify third-party components and licence conditions. Do not make an undocumented transfer between the founder and a company simply to fit a headline deal structure.

## The first commercial test

Establish whether an eligible counterparty can and wants to fund the disclosed package. Prioritise that test over building additional speculative features. Work with the actual investor or authorised decision-maker, and establish whether the money would be personal, corporate or public. A public official's interest is not authority to deploy public funds.

For each party, record:

| Question | Evidence sought |
| --- | --- |
| Who is investing and controlling the investment? | Verified legal identity, investing entity if any, ultimate beneficial owners, authorised decision-maker and any intermediaries |
| Is the transaction suitable for the planned banking/launch structure? | Transaction-specific sanctions/PEP assessment, source-of-wealth/funds documentation and receiving-bank review of the proposed parties, ownership and funding route |
| Can the party fund it? | Credible evidence of capacity for the proposed amount, payment currency, funding account and conditions; disclose sensitive evidence through the agreed professional diligence channel |
| What are they buying? | New company shares, existing founder shares, both, or selected IP/assets; proposed ownership, voting and board rights |
| Will they pay the founder secondary? | Express written response to the $8m secondary component, not an ambiguous promise of “$18m investment” |
| What must happen before closing? | Diligence, approvals, agreements, banking acceptance, funding conditions and a realistic timetable |

## Ownership, political exposure and banking access

Political or militia affiliation alone is not a finding that a person is sanctioned or that their wealth is unlawful. Determine the actual individual, role, ownership interests and applicable rules. FATF treats PEP measures as preventive and calls for additional risk controls; PEP status is not an accusation of criminality. Use identity and beneficial-owner checks, documented wealth/funds and the approval/monitoring measures required for the relationship. [FATF PEP guidance](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Peps-r12-r22.html).

Screen the actual parties and relevant owners/controllers under all sanctions regimes applicable to the entities, banks, currency and intended operations. Libya-related sanctions include targeted asset freezes. A commercial introduction or official relationship cannot replace the applicable authorisations. [UN Libya sanctions committee](https://main.un.org/securitycouncil/en/sanctions/1970), [OFAC Libya programme](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information/libya-sanctions).

The earlier 50% proposal also warrants ownership-specific review: under OFAC's rules, an entity owned 50% or more in aggregate, directly or indirectly, by blocked persons is itself blocked. A smaller stake does not establish that a direct transaction with a blocked person is permitted. A holding company or intermediary does not remove the need to identify the real owners and funds. Do not structure around a threshold to conceal or evade a restriction. [OFAC ownership guidance](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/401).

The commercial goal is **a shareholder and funding route that the relevant banks and authorities can accept**. Absence from one list is not a banking guarantee. Have independent transaction counsel/compliance assess the actual names and ownership, and have the intended receiving bank review the proposed transaction before accepting money or issuing rights. If a prohibition applies, do not proceed without any legally required authorisation; pursue an eligible counterparty instead.

## Opening transaction — founder's requested terms

The proposal below records the founder's desired ask. It is not an independent valuation or an accepted offer. It assumes the founder owns 100% of existing fully diluted common equity, with no debt/preference/option adjustments, and that primary and secondary shares have equivalent economics at the same price. Recalculate against the actual cap table and share classes.

| Term | Preferred opening proposal | Earlier 50% alternative, for comparison only |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Total investor consideration | $18,000,000 | $18,000,000 |
| Company receives for newly issued shares | $10,000,000 | $10,000,000 |
| Founder receives for existing shares | $8,000,000 gross | $8,000,000 gross |
| Investor ownership after both transactions | 40% | 50% |
| Founder ownership after both transactions | 60% | 50% |
| Implied post-money equity value | $45,000,000 | $36,000,000 |
| Implied pre-money equity value | $35,000,000 | $26,000,000 |
| Post-closing stake bought through primary funding | 22.2222% | 27.7778% |
| Post-closing stake bought from founder | 17.7778% | 22.2222% |

Keep $8m founder proceeds and $10m company funding explicit from the first price discussion. They have different recipients and purposes. Primary proceeds fund the company; secondary proceeds pay the selling shareholder. [Carta primary/secondary explanation](https://carta.com/learn/equity/liquidity-events/secondary-transactions/).

The $8m is gross, before the founder's taxes, fees and any negotiated retained amounts. If the requirement is $8m net, calculate the necessary gross payment using the actual tax residences, entities and contract. No tax rate is assumed here.

Seek cash payment of the agreed secondary at closing, with funding verified through the agreed settlement arrangements. Do not describe earnouts, rollover equity, future salary or an unfunded promise as cash received. Closing conditions and any escrow/indemnity exposure must be explicit; a cash sale does not guarantee the absence of every later liability.

Do not offer 50% merely because it is available. First obtain the investor's objection to the 40% package. Compare the additional ownership requested against actual consideration, economic preferences, governance, founder obligations and executable commercial commitments. A 50/50 structure needs an agreed board, operating authority and deadlock process. Those terms belong in the negotiation, not in a verbal assumption that equal shares will work.

## What to say in the meeting — draft only

> I own the Tazdan code/IP and can provide the technical and ownership material for diligence. I am seeking an $18m transaction comprising $10m of new capital into the company and $8m to purchase part of my existing shareholding, for 40% total post-closing ownership on the proposed same-price basis.
>
> The investment case combines the existing platform with a documented launch and partner plan. We will distinguish tested code, formal approvals and commercial contracts in the data room. We have local relationships that may help with introductions and regulatory process; formal permissions and bank arrangements will be documented separately.
>
> If this is within your investment scope, I would like a written indication covering the amount, primary/secondary split, investing party, ownership and governance requested, funding capacity, conditions and diligence timetable. We will agree the banking and compliance process for the actual counterparties before funds are accepted.

This language is an opening ask, not a representation that the platform already has the contracts, permissions or earnings needed to justify $45m. Do not disclose another party's identity or suggest that unnamed interest is a competing funded bid.

## Turn the CBL relationship into execution evidence

Give the contact a defined introduction/process role and document any commercial engagement. Ask for these concrete results through the appropriate official channels:

1. A meeting with the responsible CBL team and a written record of the proposed entity, activities, application route, owner/controller review, capital and documentation requirements. Distinguish wallet/payment services, FX and cross-border remittances.
2. Introductions to suitable licensed banks/providers, followed by their written requirements, commercial proposal and willingness to onboard the actual entity and disclosed owners.
3. A corridor-specific path for the initial overseas partner, funding, FX access and settlement, with exact accounts, obligations and reconciliation. Local access does not establish permissions or partners in every destination country.
4. A task register with institution, named responsible role, required evidence, dependency, cost and expected decision date. Record expected dates as estimates until the institution commits.

Do not value the company as if all these outcomes have already happened. Use written progress to strengthen the investment case as it is obtained. CBL publicly distinguishes approved service types and company statuses in its [electronic-payment directory](https://cbl.gov.ly/en/electronic-payment/); an individual's relationship is not an entry or approval for Tazdan.

| Timing | Action | Decision evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–3 | Prepare confidential technical/ownership pack; identify investor decision-makers, actual investing parties and funding type; engage receiving-bank/compliance channel | Counterparty and funds review can start; no eligibility finding is assumed |
| Days 4–7 | Once suitable for discussion, hold individual meetings and clearly present the $18m primary/secondary ask | Written response to price, $8m founder proceeds, ownership, governance and conditions |
| Days 8–14 | Compare eligible, credible responses; progress diligence and official launch introductions | Written indication/term sheet if achievable, funding evidence, named diligence owners and next decision dates |

This is a work schedule, not a promise of bank clearance, a term sheet or closing within two weeks. Detailed screening may take longer. Continue the [institutional software sales track](TAZDAN_TECH_MONETIZATION_PLAN_2026_10_17.md) in parallel; it provides commercial evidence and an alternative source of company revenue.

## Decision rule

Prefer an eligible, funded counterparty with transparent ownership, documented funds, acceptable governance and a clearly separated founder payment. A proposed cheque that prevents the intended bank/provider relationships would undermine the financial-services business. The next useful result is a qualified written response to the actual price—not a higher assumed probability based on wealth or influence.
